For decades, artificial intelligence advanced in small steps. Expert systems in
the 1980s encoded human knowledge as hand-written rules, and they were brittle:
every new situation needed a new rule, written by a person.

Three things changed the picture. First, the internet produced training data at
a scale no lab could have collected by hand. Second, graphics processors made
it cheap to train large neural networks. Third, the transformer architecture,
published in 2017, gave models a better way to weigh which words matter in a
sentence.

The pattern repeated everywhere it was tried. Machine translation, image
recognition, and speech systems all jumped forward when they were given more
data and more compute, with the same basic recipe. Scale plus attention beat
clever hand-coded rules, every single time.
